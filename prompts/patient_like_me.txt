Each candidate was retrieved by one or more similarity strategies:
- **Limb threat**: matched on WIfI grades and stage, tissue loss, foot infection, haemodynamics
- **Anatomy**: matched on disease segment distribution, GLASS stage, runoff and target vessel
- **Trajectory**: matched on revascularisation history, strategy, conduit, reinterventions and amputations
- **Narrative**: matched on clinical narrative similarity (BM25)

## YOUR TASK
Evaluate each candidate for TRUE clinical similarity to the index patient.

**Clinical similarity means:**
- Same limb threat severity (WIfI stage, tissue loss extent, infection, perfusion)
- Comparable anatomical disease pattern (segments involved, runoff, available target vessel)
- Comparable treatment decision point (same revascularisation line, same clinical question)
- A vascular board would consider them "similar cases" worth comparing

**For each candidate, assess:**
1. **Limb threat and perfusion alignment**: Do they share WIfI stage, tissue loss extent, infection status and comparable toe pressure / ABI / TcPO2? Limb threat severity is the strongest signal - two WIfI stage 4 limbs with single-vessel runoff are more similar than two patients with matching demographics.
2. **Anatomical alignment**: Same disease segments (aortoiliac / femoropopliteal / infrapopliteal), comparable GLASS stage, comparable target vessel and runoff quality?
3. **Treatment trajectory alignment**: Are they at the same point in their journey? Same number of prior revascularisations on the index limb, same strategy (endovascular / open / hybrid), similar conduit availability, prior minor or major amputation?
4. **Key differences**: What clinically meaningful differences exist? Diabetes, dialysis / CKD, ambulatory status, cardiac fitness, age, prior treatments that diverge?

**Select the 1-5 BEST clinical twins.** Not every candidate will be a good match - it is acceptable to select fewer if only 1-2 are truly relevant. For each selected twin, provide:
- **rationale**: WHY they are a good match (cite specific shared features)
- **key_differences**: What's NOT the same (clinically meaningful differences only)
- **trajectory_summary**: Their treatment journey in brief
- **similarity_rating**: "Strong Twin" (near-identical decision point, limb threat and anatomy) or "Partial Twin" (shared limb threat but different anatomy or trajectory, or vice versa)
- **relevance_to_decision**: How does this patient's experience inform the index patient's treatment decision?
